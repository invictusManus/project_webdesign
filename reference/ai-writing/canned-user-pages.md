Why it matters for web copy: the template is a generic "About me" profile page. The same skeleton, section titles and closing invitation turn up on personal sites, team pages and contact pages. The long table of example user pages at the end of the source is not reproduced.

## Wikipedia:Canned user pages
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Canned_user_pages

### Lead: the canned template
- Many user pages follow one pattern. The page gives a fictional example with: a level 1 heading with a waving-hand emoji and `Welcome to My User Page!`; a quotation in quotes (`"Information wants to be free."`); sections `About Me`, `My Interests`, `How I Contribute`, `Goals`, `Let's Connect!`, each often with its own emoji (👤 🌍 📝 🎯 💬); bulleted lists; bold on the talk-page link; a horizontal rule; a closing quotation attributed to a person; `Thank you for visiting. Let's work together to preserve freely accessible knowledge for future generations.`
- Typical sentences in the example: `believe in helping spread free knowledge in a manner that aligns with Wikipedia's goals`; `Citing reliable sources to support information`; `Fixing formatting to ensure readability and neutrality`; `Doing cleanup to ensure adherence to editorial guidelines`; `I can't wait to collaborate with everyone to make Wikipedia and the world a better place!`
- Variations exist. Many use title case, lists, emoji and bold. Sometimes Markdown or "feel free to customize it further"-style leftovers appear (strong giveaways) and are removed in later revisions.

### Features
- Features below do not guarantee AI. Newer pages may imitate older ones; newcomers may not know the conventions. Being cited for one feature is not an accusation unless strong indicators (Markdown, such as `**bold**` or `#` headings) are present. Even Markdown is not absolute (habit from workplaces that require AI use).
- The page's advice: avoid AI for article content and comments; how a user page looks is the least concern as long as AI text is not passed off as one's own.
#### Generic ethos statements
- Sections like `How I Contribute` or `Why I'm on Wikipedia` that list standards the user will follow (neutrality, verifiability, reliable sourcing). Chatbots know nothing about the user, so they describe an ideal editor: expanding stubs, fixing typos, finding sources. Generic planned-editing text also appears in unblock requests and is unconvincing.
- Examples: `As someone who writes professionally, I naturally spend time looking for credible references`; `Respecting the rules around neutrality, verifiability, and citations`; `I'm here as a quiet contributor, not to stand out, but to stand by Wikipedia's mission`; `I prefer doing my homework before making changes. Wikipedia isn't a playground, it's a platform that works because people respect the process`; `My goal is to help make Wikipedia a more trustworthy and useful source of knowledge for readers around the world`; headings `Editing Principles`, `Accuracy`, `Verifiability`, `Neutrality`, `Reliable sourcing`, `Respect for the Wikipedia community`.
#### Invitation to use talk page
- Sections titled `Let's Connect!`, `Let's Collaborate!` or just `Contact`, with wording that invites messages (emoji 📬 💬 🤝 may precede them). Example phrasings: `I'm always excited to connect with fellow Wikipedia editors and enthusiasts`; `Feel free to drop me a message on my talk page if you'd like to discuss ...`; `Feel free to leave a message on my **Talk Page** if you have any suggestions, questions, or would like to collaborate. Happy Editing! 🚀`; `Want to collaborate, share ideas, or just say hi? I'd love to hear from you!`; `I'm always open to learning from others and sharing what I know`; `Let's work together to enhance the world's largest encyclopedia!`.
- Search phrases noted: `I'm always happy to`, `I'm always open to`, `Feel free to leave a message on my talk page`.
#### Vague conflict of interest statements
- A section pledging to avoid conflicts or to disclose them "if needed", without saying what the conflict is. Examples: `I fully support Wikipedia's commitment to neutrality and avoid editing any articles where I have a conflict of interest`; `If I contribute to or comment on a topic related to organizations I may be affiliated with ... I will disclose that relationship clearly`; `I disclose that I may request edits ... on behalf of clients`.
#### Creator-facing messages
- Messages aimed at the page's creator instead of readers. Examples: `Here's an updated version of your Wikipedia-style profile, including a section for your projects.`; `Feel free to personalize the tone or add your achievements. Want me to add any links, pictures, or favorite quotes?`; `Here's a template for your wiki user page. You can copy and paste this onto your user page and customize it further.`; `Here's a draft you can use for your Wikipedia user page that keeps it professional, transparent, and in line with Wikipedia's tone guidelines`.
#### Formatting and style
##### Attempted transclusion of nonexistent userboxes
- Userbox calls that do not exist (red links), for example `Template:User Research`, `Template:User learning`, `Template:User contributes in good faith`, `User:UBX/Coffee`.
##### Emoji in userboxes
- Emoji in a userbox id parameter (flags, hedgehog, laptop, music note). Possibly just aesthetics, so weak alone.
##### Unclosed tables encasing the sections below them
- A table never closed swallows every later section into one cell. Examples show star-rating bars (`★★★★★`) for "Article creation", "Copy-editing" and similar, and two-column "Area / Contribution" and "Project / Interests" tables.
##### Text boxes with round corners
- Content in rounded outline or gradient boxes, often with emoji headings and tagline text; users copy the look from other pages; edit summaries may say "New style". Example boxes: `📊 Market Analysis`, `💰 Personal Finance Basics`, small uppercase category labels.
#### Miscellaneous
- Common points not about style: many centre on a hobby or culture; some are translated from another language; many belong to self-described software engineers or IT staff, often with an AI interest (probably because they are likelier to generate such a page).
- Be very careful judging by content. Content is not incriminating on its own. It matters when combined with more reliable indicators (a generic outline of what the user will do, titles common among canned pages such as `Let's Connect!`, or Markdown).
