## Wikipedia:Marketing buzzspeak
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Marketing_buzzspeak

### Summary line
- "Do not use marketing buzzspeak on Wikipedia."

### Definition
- Marketing buzzspeak: communication methods meant to persuade, convince, sell or dazzle without conveying extra meaning. Found in business and in other areas such as academia. An encyclopedia is neutral and should not contain it.

### Examples
#### Business world
- Words that add no content: `thought leader`, `influencer`, `trend-setter`, `cutting edge` (for a person, company or product). If a critic said it, it is still empty praise.
- Where a term is well defined, use the plainer standard term (the page's example: "internet celebrity" instead of "influencer"), though avoiding both is preferable.
- `award-winning` alone is meaningless. List the awards with references. Awards vary in value; anyone can create one, and some industries (book publishing, spirits) have many vanity awards used as advertising. An award is only significant if the encyclopedia has an article about it, and it should be reported with a reliable source.
#### Academia
- `methodology` is often misused to make a "method" sound more sophisticated. (Methodology is properly the study of methods.)

### Cleanup
- Pages with buzzspeak often have no useful content. If so, tag for speedy deletion as advertising. If some signal exists, judge salvageability; usually it is better to start over ("blow it up"). Salvageable article-space pages can move to drafts; often they should be deleted. Repeatedly re-created pages may need the title protected.
#### Cleanup of draft
- A draft with buzzspeak may be declined, rejected or tagged. If declined, do not ask reviewers which parts are buzzspeak so you can remove them: that asks the reviewer to write the article and often signals a conflict of interest. Declare the conflict and rework the draft neutrally yourself.

### Marketing buzzspeak and artificial intelligence
- Some essays and papers written by LLMs contain buzzspeak, and some pages with buzzspeak are suspected of LLM authorship. Buzzspeak predates LLMs by centuries. It does not matter whether a nominated page came from an LLM: if it is mostly buzzspeak, that is reason enough to delete.
- Advice to authors: if other humans suspect your work is machine-made, review or change your writing style.
